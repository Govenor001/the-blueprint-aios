# Demo business — "Brightside Bookkeeping"

Stuck on a day because you don't have real data handy? Run the whole
challenge as Maya, the fictional owner of Brightside Bookkeeping. Paste
these answers into `blueprint-intake.md` (or read them to `/blueprint`)
and every skill in the kit works end to end.

## Q1 — Who are you, what do you sell, who do you sell it to?
I'm Maya Chen, a solo bookkeeper in Portland. I sell monthly bookkeeping
($400–$900/mo) and quarterly cleanup projects ($1,500 flat) to service
businesses with 2–15 employees — trades, clinics, agencies. They come to
me when tax season scares them.

## Q2 — Voice samples (pasted raw)
Sample 1 (email to a client):
"Hey Dan — books are closed for March. Two things: your fuel spend is up
40% vs Jan and there's a duplicate sub for that scheduling app, $89/mo.
Want me to cancel the old one? Also your quarterly is due June 15, I'll
have numbers to you by the 5th."

Sample 2 (a post):
"Unpopular opinion: your bookkeeper shouldn't surprise you in April.
If you're finding out in tax season what your margins were in August,
you don't have a bookkeeping problem, you have a reporting problem."

## Q3 — 90-day priorities
1. Sign 4 new monthly clients (currently 11, want 15) by Nov 15.
2. Launch a 500-subscriber email list — one useful email every two weeks.
3. Cut my own admin (invoicing, chasing documents) from 6 hrs/wk to 2.

## Q4 — Where does revenue land?
Stripe invoices; tracked in a Google Sheet called "Pipeline & MRR".

## Q5 — Where do you talk to customers?
Gmail, almost entirely. Occasional texts from two older clients.

## Q6 — Where do notes and docs live?
Google Drive, one folder per client. Meeting notes in Google Docs.

## Q7 — What one task eats your week, and where is work tracked?
Chasing clients for receipts and statements — every single month.
Tasks live in a Google Tasks list, badly.
